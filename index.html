Forest Loss Risk Prediction: Southern Rockies

2025–2026 · Python · Google Earth Engine · XGBoost · Optuna · MapLibre

Question: for forest that is still standing at the end of a given year, how likely is it to lose tree cover in the next 1, 2 or 3 years? The study area is the southern Rockies along the BC–Alberta border.

How it works: I build features in Google Earth Engine from Landsat surface reflectance, nearby recent loss, fire history and terrain, then label each location using the Hansen Global Forest Change dataset. One XGBoost model is trained per time horizon. Hyperparameters are tuned with Optuna, and the probabilities are calibrated so they mean what they say. The model then scores a 1 km grid of the whole region, and the results are shown on a static web map.

Validation: I put most of the effort here. Nearby pixels and nearby years look alike, so a random train/test split would give inflated scores. Instead, the data is split both by time (train on 2003–2014, test on 2019 onward) and by space (20% of ~15 km blocks are never seen in training). The metrics are reweighted to the real forest population, and the model is compared against simple baselines.

Result: forest loss is rare (about 1.5% per year), so I judge the model by how well it ranks risk. Among the 1% of locations it rates riskiest, loss happens 10.7× more often than average. The best simple baseline (distance to recent loss) manages 2.3×.

Startup Exit Signals

2026 · Python · pandas · Jupyter

Question: which company and funding characteristics go along with a startup being acquired or going public?

How it works: using a historical Crunchbase-style dataset, I filtered the records to about 196,000 companies, merged in the acquisition and IPO tables, and created outcome labels (acquired, IPO, any exit). I then compared exit rates by sector, total funding by outcome, and exit rates by number of funding rounds.

Findings: technical sectors had the highest exit rates (semiconductors 30%, against 7% for web). The median IPO company raised $25M; the median closed company raised $0.65M. Exit rates rose from 8% for companies with one funding round to 20% for those with seven or more. The write-up is clear about the limits: the data ends around 2014, and these are associations, not causes.

LingoLens: Lip Reading with Deep Learning

2024 · Python · TensorFlow / Keras · OpenCV

What it does: LingoLens takes a silent video of someone speaking and predicts the sentence from their lip movements alone. It's a university project built with a classmate. The model follows the LipNet architecture (University of Oxford and Google DeepMind), and our implementation started from a public LipNet tutorial in TensorFlow.

How it works: each video is split into frames, the mouth region is cropped with OpenCV, and the frames are converted to grayscale and normalised. 3D (spatiotemporal) convolutions pick up the shape and motion of the lips across frames. Bidirectional LSTM layers then read the sequence in both directions and turn it into characters, trained with CTC loss. The model was trained on the GRID audio-visual sentence corpus.

Result: 99% word accuracy on 100 test videos. That number comes from a single-speaker setup (one GRID speaker), so it shows the pipeline works rather than that it generalises to new faces.

Salus: Food Ingredient Checker Hack the Change 2025

2025 · Next.js · TypeScript · Supabase · Python · Open Food Facts

Built at Hack the Change 2025. What it does: users save their allergens and the ingredients they want to avoid, then scan a product's barcode or a menu item's QR code. The app looks up the ingredients and flags any that match their list, along with whether the item is vegan or vegetarian.

How it works: a Next.js front end with two views, one for users and one for food facilities that publish their menu items. API routes call a Python scanner, look up products in the Open Food Facts database, and store preferences and menus in Supabase.

TaxRAG: Canadian Income Tax Research Assistant In progress

2026 · Python · PostgreSQL + pgvector · Claude API

Goal: an assistant that answers personal federal income tax questions and cites its sources for every answer: the Income Tax Act, the Regulations, CRA Income Tax Folios and CRA guides. Dollar amounts that change each year (rates, RRSP/TFSA limits) come from a structured table, so the language model never has to recall a number from memory.

Done so far: a reproducible corpus download with a provenance record, and a parser that splits the Act and Regulations into about 9,400 citable units and 1,900 defined terms. Next: chunking the guides, hybrid (keyword + vector) retrieval, answer generation with citation checks, and an evaluation set.
